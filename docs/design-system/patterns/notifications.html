Pattern

Notifications

Telo Ride tells the rider things in three ways. A banner carries page-level news: a map just opened, or this browser can't reach the bike. An inline status sits beside what changed: a seat opened in the lobby. A toast confirms something that happened out of view. The mocks need no toast yet; the rules below are for when one does.

Overview

Telo Ride has no accounts, no inbox and no notification centre: everything happens on the rider's machine, in the moment. So a notification is always about this screen, and it lives as long as it is true. The unlock banner greets the rider on Home right after a map opens; the browser notice stays under the header for as long as the browser can't do Bluetooth; the lobby notice sits by the start grid while the seat is open.

Components used: Alert and banner, Toast, Inline message, Button and Badge.

Priority

  1. Blocking (the bike is gone): an overlay, role="alert", focus moves to its action.
  2. Limiting (can browse, can't ride): a warning banner under the header, role="alert", persistent.
  3. Celebrating (a map opened): a gold banner at the top of the content, role="status".
  4. Contextual (a seat opened): inline status beside the thing, role="status".
  5. Confirming (done out of view): a toast, role="status".

Higher priority wins the space: while the bike is lost, nothing else is announced. Two notices of the same level stack in arrival order; never more than two toasts at once.

Toast

The region is always in the page (.toast-region, empty), so screen readers already know it when a toast arrives. A toast holds an icon, one or two lines, at most one action, and a close button.

Accessibility

Live regions

  • The toast region and every status container exist when the page loads; content is injected into them. A live region created at the same moment as its message is often not announced.
  • Polite (role="status"): the unlock banner, the lobby notice, toasts. Assertive (role="alert"): the browser notice, error toasts, Lost the bike.
  • Notifications never take focus. The browser notice's button is the first control after the header, so it is one Tab away.
  • A toast's action must also exist somewhere permanent; a toast is never the only way to do something.

Timing

Auto-dismissing toasts stay at least 8 seconds and pause while hovered or focused (2.2.1). Errors never auto-dismiss. Banners don't time out at all.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReach a banner's action in reading order; reach a toast's Dismiss (the toast pauses while focused).
Enter / SpaceRun the action or dismiss.
EscDismiss the focused toast.

Contrast

Unlock title on gold tint
Warning title on warning tint
Danger title on danger tint
Lobby notice on raised

Motion

Toasts slide up over --duration-base and fade out; under reduced motion they only fade. Banners appear without motion.

Touch

Toast Dismiss is 36px (.icon-btn); banner actions are 36px small buttons.

Content

  • Banners are sentences with a full stop: “Neon Docklands is open.” Detail follows in muted text: “A harbour city after dark, 21.6 km.”
  • A warning names the fix first: “Open Telo Ride in Chrome or Edge.”, then why, then what still works: “you can look around, but not ride.”
  • Inline status says who and what: “MarcoVelo left. Their seat is open again.”
  • Toasts are past tense and say what changed, in the rider's numbers: “Ride saved.”
  • Actions are two or three words: Ride it, Copy link, Open over HTTPS.
  • No exclamation marks, no “Success”, no “Congratulations”. The unlock is news, stated plainly; gold does the celebrating.
