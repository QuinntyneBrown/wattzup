Content and tone

The Content foundation holds the rules and the full microcopy library. This page shows them working together on real screens: what each line of a card is for, how copy changes as the rider moves from choosing to riding to finishing, and how to check a new screen before it ships.

Overview

Telo Ride talks like a coach standing next to the bike: short, second person, specific, calm when something breaks. Rules for single strings (sentence case, curly apostrophes, units, dates) live in Content. A screen adds a second layer: each line has one job, the lines are read in order, and the rider's state (choosing, riding, broken, finished) decides how many words they can take.

Components used: Next ride, Photo tag, Button, HUD panel, Alert and banner, Dialog, Leaderboard and Empty state.

A fault: what happened, what's safe, what to do

Lost the bike

Echelon EX-5s stopped answering. Your rider coasts for 15 seconds while we reconnect; after that the race counts as a DNF.

pages/race/disconnected

  1. Title: the fact, in plain words. “Lost the bike”, not “Connection error”.
  2. First sentence: which thing. Names the bike (Echelon EX-5s), so a rider with two bikes knows.
  3. Second: what is safe and for how long. “Your rider coasts for 15 seconds” and the honest cost after that.
  4. Action: one verb. Reconnect. No Cancel; leaving is in the header.

The same formula writes “Bluetooth is off” (Turn on Bluetooth on this computer, then try again · Try again) and the unsupported-browser notice. See Empty and error states for every case.

Responsive behaviour

  • Copy is written for 360 first. Button labels fit one line at 360 with btn--block; “Save ride and open library” is the longest and it fits.
  • Truncate facts, never instructions. Map facts and goals may end in an ellipsis at 360 (“Finish top 3 vs Bots on Old To…”) because the full goal is one tap away; a button label, title or error never truncates.
  • Hide the extra sentence, not the action. Below 640px the browser notice drops its icon, not its words.
  • Compact forms only where width is short: “5/10” on cards and the start grid, “6 of 10 riders” in sentences; “5th / 10” only in the HUD.

Accessibility

  • Labels read alone. Every button and link makes sense out of context in a screen reader's list: “Race Old Town Criterium”, not “Go”; “Close diagnostics”, not “Close”.
  • Hidden words complete visual shorthand. A dash becomes “No best yet”; a lock becomes “, locked”; stars become “1 of 3 stars”.
  • Uppercase is CSS, so overlines and TELO are not spelled out letter by letter.
  • Plain language: short sentences, common words, no jargon (“reconnect”, not “re-establish GATT session”). Device ids and dBm live in Diagnostics, for people who ask.
  • Announced copy is short. Text in role="status" and role="alert" regions is read in full on every change, so it stays to one or two sentences.
  • Numbers carry units in text, not only in a visually separate span, so “73 kJ” is read as one thing.

Content

Checklist for a new screen

  1. Can each line's job be named in three words? Cut lines that can't.
  2. Is the rider “you”, and is every map, bike and class named?
  3. Does the primary button start with a verb and read alone?
  4. Are numbers in the foundation's formats (27:10, 14.2 km, +0:07, 2nd of 10)?
  5. For a fault: what happened, what's safe, what to do, one action?
  6. For an empty space: what will appear here, and what puts it there?
  7. Sentence case, curly apostrophes, no exclamation marks, no “OK” or “Submit”?
  8. Is it real copy, about something the product does today? Never placeholder text, never a promise of a later feature.
