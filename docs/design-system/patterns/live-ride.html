Pattern

Live ride

Every ride screen, whether a world race, a video class or a custom plan, is one composition: something to look at, one big number, the current interval, a way to change resistance, and an honest answer when the bike or the ride stops.

Overview

There are three ride screens and they share one structure:

ScreenBackdropPrimary numberPlanOthersResistance
World Ride (race)The 3D world (.race-scene).race-power.race-interval + .race-route.race-board.race-resist
Class rideThe class video (.class-video).class-stat tiles.class-segment + .class-profileOutput board.class-resist
Custom rideSynced video or the trace (.ride-view).ride-readouts.ride-interval + .ride-traceNone.ride-control

Parts: HUD panel, Ride readout, Route profile, Plan chart, Ride trace, Countdown, Leaderboard.

When to use

  • Use it for any screen where the rider is pedalling and the clock is running. Everything else (picking a class, setting up a plan, results) is an ordinary page.
  • Use the world composition when there is a map; the video composition when there is a class; the dashboard when the rider built the plan.
  • Don't add a fourth layout for a new ride type: pick the nearest of the three and swap the backdrop.
  • Don't put navigation, settings or anything that needs reading on a ride screen. The app bar is gone; the only ways out are Finish and Leave (or End).

  1. Backdrop on --z-base. Nothing interactive. It fills the viewport (the race) or the left column (class video, custom view).
  2. Bar at the top: lockup, where you are ("Old Town Criterium · Cathedral Circuit · vs Bots, medium"), the bike, the clock ("5:01 / 30:00"), then the ride's actions: Pause (solo only), Finish, Leave or End, Full screen.
  3. Primary number top left: watts against target, with position and gaps.
  4. Others top right: standings or "You vs your best".
  5. Plan and controls along the bottom: interval (what now, how long, what next), route or profile (where in the ride), resistance (the one thing you press).
  6. Overlays on --z-overlay: paused, lost the bike, the 3-2-1.
  7. One announcer: a visually hidden .ride-announcer (role="status") for milestones.

No bike

Races and classes cannot start without a bike: the lobby and Class Start ask first. The custom ride can open without one and shows the plan with an overlay ("Connect your bike", or "Turn on Bluetooth" with Check again); readouts show "—" with their units, and the log says "Nothing sent — no bike connected." See HUD panel overlays.

Starting and finishing

Races start from the lobby's clock and a 3-2-1 (Countdown); classes from Class Start's "Join · starts in 9 min". Finish goes to Results; End saves and goes to Profile.

Responsive behaviour

The structure above follows this page's viewport: open it at 360px to see the phone race.

  • 360px: one number and one interval. The race keeps the bar, the power panel and the interval; standings, route and resistance are hidden, and the plan drives resistance. The class ride stacks video, then tiles two by two, then the board. The custom ride stacks telemetry over the control. Bar and header buttons are 48px.
  • 640px: the race adds the route panel; class tiles go four across.
  • 768px: the race adds standings.
  • 1024px: the race's bottom HUD becomes three columns with resistance; the class ride and custom ride become two columns (content plus a 280px board or 340px control).
  • Landscape phones get the 640px layout. Full screen is offered on every ride screen; Esc leaves it.

Accessibility

What updates live

ThingHow oftenAnnounced?
Watts, cadence, heart, speed, distance, energy, gapsEvery second (with the bike's notifications)No. Readable on demand.
Ride clock, interval time left, lobby clockEvery secondNo. role="timer", implicit aria-live="off".
Standings orderEvery secondOnly a place change that holds for 5 s: "Up to 4th".
New intervalAt each boundaryPolitely: "Work, 30 seconds at level 24".
ResistanceOn a press or a plan changePolitely: "Resistance 25 of 32".
Paused, resumedOn changePolitely.
Lost the bikeOn changeAssertively (role="alert"), and focus moves to Reconnect.
3-2-1Each second for 3 sAssertively.

One .ride-announcer per screen, at most one message every 5 seconds; a newer message replaces a queued one.

Keyboard and focus

Tab order follows reading order: bar actions, then the power panel (no stops), then resistance, then the board (no stops). Everything mid-ride is a native button. Under an overlay the HUD is inert. Focus is never moved by a live update.

Touch

Anything pressed mid-ride is at least 48px (--target-ride): resistance steps everywhere, and bar and header buttons on phones. Desktop headers keep the design's 36–40px (open question 9) pending a decision.

Motion

The world itself moves; the HUD does not. Under prefers-reduced-motion the 3-2-1 cuts instead of scaling, standings stop sliding, and the world's camera shake is off. Clocks keep ticking: they are timers, not decoration.

Content

  • The bar names the place, not the mode: "Old Town Criterium", then the route and opponent.
  • Overlay titles in three words or fewer; bodies say what happens to the ride ("counts as a DNF", "saved at 6:52", "keeps going").
  • Numbers carry units; signs use U+2212; times are m:ss.
  • Never praise or scold mid-ride ("Great job!", "Too slow"). "On target" and "−47 W" are enough.
