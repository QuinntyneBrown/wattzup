Overview

Every word on this page comes from the mocks. Telo Ride's copy is short because the rider is reading it between efforts, second person because it is about them, and specific because a coach who says “nice work” is less use than one who says “Finish top 3 vs Bots on Old Town Criterium”.

Use this page to write new screens, notices and labels. The Content and tone pattern shows the same rules applied to whole screens.

Voice and tone

Evening, Sam.

Old Town Criterium · 14.2 km · Easy
Home greets the rider by name and time of day, then gets straight to the next ride.
Short

One idea per sentence; fragments are fine where a label would be.

Pedal once to wake it up.
Second person

Talk to the rider about their ride. “You”, “your best”, never “the user”.

Your best here: 27:10
Coach-like

Say what to do next and why it matters. Encourage with facts, not exclamation marks.

Keep pedalling easy. Resistance is set for the first stretch.
Specific

Name the map, the bike, the number. Vague praise is noise.

A top 3 here earns the second star.
Honest about limits

Say what will not work and what will, without blame.

Safari and Firefox don’t support Web Bluetooth yet.
Calm under failure

When something breaks mid-ride, lead with what is safe.

Your ride is saved at 6:52.

Capitalisation and punctuation

  • Sentence case everywhere: titles, buttons, labels, tabs, notices. “Connect your bike”, “Send feedback”, “Recent rides”, “Go to the start line”.
  • Proper names keep their capitals: maps (Old Town Criterium, Neon Docklands), the bike (Echelon EX-5s), classes (30 Minute HIIT Cardio), Bluetooth, Chrome, Edge, HTTPS.
  • Uppercase is styling, not text. The wordmark TELO and overlines are written in sentence case and uppercased by CSS (.wide, text-transform), so screen readers do not spell them out.
  • Rider handles stay as typed: lena.k, MarcoVelo, priya_spins.
  • Full stops end sentences in body text and notices (“Open Telo Ride in Chrome or Edge.”) but not single-line labels, buttons or titles (“Lost the bike”).
  • Curly apostrophes and quotes: can’t, you’re, “Leave this ride?”. Never straight ' in copy.
  • Spaced em dash for an aside: “Thanks — we’re on it.” · “Following the plan — levels change with each interval.”
  • Middle dot separates facts on one line: “Old Town Criterium · 14.2 km · Easy”.
  • Ellipsis only for something in progress: “Pairing…”, “Looking for bikes nearby…”. One character (…), not three dots.
  • No exclamation marks. Not even for a podium.

Buttons and links

A button starts with a verb and, where it helps, names the thing it acts on. The rider should know what happens before pressing.

PatternExamples from the mocks
Verb + the thingRace Old Town Criterium · Pair Echelon EX-5s · Ride Neon Docklands · Download packet log · Copy link · Open over HTTPS
Verb + whereGo to the start line · Back to Home · Join the next session
Verb alone, when context is the thingReconnect · Pause · Resume · Finish · Leave · End · Done · Check again · Try again
Two outcomes, both explicitSave ride and open library · Discard and open library · Keep riding
AgainRace it again · Ride it again · Send another
Commitment in first personI’m ready (the start line, where the rider is declaring something)
Links that navigateAll maps · All classes · edit workout
  • Never “OK”, “Submit”, “Click here” or “Yes / No”. “Leave this ride?” is answered by what each choice does.
  • In a busy state the label says what is happening: “Pairing…”, with the button marked aria-disabled="true" and aria-busy="true" so focus stays on it (never disabled, which drops focus).
  • A countdown inside a button keeps the verb first: “Join · starts in 9 min”.

Dates and times

  • Clock times in 12-hour with a space and capital PM: “7:39 PM”, “7:54 PM”. Follow the rider's locale when the product localises.
  • Recent days are relative or a short weekday: “Yesterday”, “Mon”, “Sun”. Older rides use the date in the rider's locale.
  • Countdowns to a start are words: “starts in 9 min”, “Group in 6 min”, “Next one starts 15 minutes later”.
  • Machine dates (file names, logs) are ISO 8601: telo-ride-packets-2024-06-14.jsonl.
  • Greetings follow the clock: “Evening, Sam.” at 7:30 PM.

Error messages

What happened+What to do (and why, if it helps)+One action

Lead with the plain fact in the title, put the fix in the body, and offer one button that does the fix. Never blame the rider, never show a code, and say what is still safe when a ride is involved.

Bluetooth is off

Turn on Bluetooth on this computer, then try again.

Open Telo Ride in Chrome or Edge. Bluetooth to the bike only works there — you can look around, but not ride.

Do and don't

Accessibility

  • Labels for icons and numbers are full words: “Lower resistance”, “Level 7, 1840 of 2600 XP”, “Elapsed 6:52 of 30:00”, “29 seconds left in this effort”, “1 of 3 stars”.
  • Link and button text stands alone (2.4.4): “Ride Neon Docklands”, not “Go”.
  • Hidden status words complete what colour shows: the header's bike name carries a visually hidden “connected”.
  • Symbols are read aloud by screen readers: write “2 stars” in labels, keep ★ for visible goals only, and avoid → in names.
  • Plain language: no jargon in notices (“Bluetooth is blocked on plain http”, not “Insecure context”).

Tokens

Content has no tokens. Type for copy is on Typography; the numeral style (.numeral, tabular figures) is there too.
