Foundation

Accessibility

Everything in the system meets WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes. Riding adds its own rules: big targets for sweaty hands, numbers that update without shouting, and notices that interrupt only when the ride is at risk.

Overview

Telo Ride commits to WCAG 2.2 level AA in both themes, on every screen, at every width from 320 px. The tables below are the system's commitments: each row names the success criterion, the rule every component follows, and what it means in this product. Component pages answer the same questions for themselves under Accessibility; the contract at the end says what each must cover.

Riding changes the usual assumptions. The rider is moving, breathing hard, often a metre from the screen, with sweat on their fingers and music in their ears. So the system adds three rules of its own on top of AA:

  • Big targets mid-ride. Anything pressed while riding is 48 px (--target-ride).
  • Numbers stay quiet. Watts, cadence, heart rate and clocks change every second; they are never announced on their own.
  • Interrupt only when the ride is at risk. “Lost the bike”, “Bluetooth is off” and the browser notices are alerts. Everything else waits to be read.

Pause only in solo rides

Open question 8 in the mocks README: the redesign plan says a solo ride can be paused and a race cannot. The mocks follow it. Solo races, solo classes and the custom ride show Pause, and the paused state freezes the clock, the world and the video (“The world holds still until you pedal again.”). Races against bots or people and group classes have no Pause: everyone shares one clock, which makes them real-time events under the 2.2.1 exception. What they do instead:

  • Leave is always one press away, and a race shows how long the rider can coast before it counts as a DNF (“Your rider coasts for 15 seconds while we reconnect”).
  • Group classes keep going and say so: “The class keeps going. Pedal to wake the bike and you’ll rejoin where everyone is.”
  • Every class is also offered solo (“Ride alone · Start now. Pause whenever you like.”), so a rider who needs breaks always has a version they can pause.

Targets while riding

Steppers in the race and the class are 48 px. The ride headers' Finish, Pause, Leave and End are the design's 36–40 px from 640 px up and 48 px below it on the custom ride (open question 9); new mid-ride controls take --target-ride at every width. See Responsive.

Focus ring

One ring, everywhere: a solid outline in --color-focus-ring, --focus-ring-width thick, drawn --focus-ring-offset outside the element so the canvas shows between the ring and a volt button. It appears on :focus-visible only, so a mouse click does not leave it behind. Text inputs draw it at offset 0, on their border. The ring colour is signal orange on ink and the deeper --palette-signal-deep on paper (orange read 2.7:1 there); its value lives on Color, and the box-shadow form --shadow-focus on Elevation.

:focus-visible { outline: var(--focus-ring-width) solid var(--color-focus-ring); outline-offset: var(--focus-ring-offset); }

Testing procedure

  1. Contrast: python3 .claude/skills/extracting-design-systems/scripts/check_contrast.py docs/design-system/tokens/tokens.css checks every pair in both themes; it must exit 0.
  2. Structure: check_design_system.py docs/design-system checks labels, names, alt text, landmarks, one <h1>, links and raw colours.
  3. Keyboard: Tab through every example on a component page and through one ride: Home → Race Setup → Lobby → Race → Results, using only the keyboard. The ring must always be visible and never hidden behind a HUD panel.
  4. Screen reader: VoiceOver with Safari or NVDA with Chrome on one dialog (“Leave this ride?”), one form (Custom workout) and one live screen (Race): check that the clock and watts stay quiet and “Lost the bike” is announced.
  5. Zoom: 200% and 400% at 1280 px; no sideways scroll, nothing clipped.
  6. Preferences: emulate prefers-reduced-motion, prefers-contrast: more and forced-colors: active in the browser's rendering tools; check both themes.
  7. Touch: ride one custom ride on a phone and press Pause, End and the steppers mid-effort.

Component page contract

Every component page's Accessibility section has these seven parts, in this order:

  1. Role and pattern: the element or ARIA pattern, linked to the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices pattern.
  2. Keyboard: a table of keys and what they do.
  3. Focus: where focus starts, where it goes on close, how the ring looks.
  4. Labelling: what must be labelled, how, and what is announced.
  5. Contrast: the token pairs with live ratios in both themes.
  6. Motion: what animates and what happens with reduced motion.
  7. Touch: target sizes, including the ride target if it is pressed mid-ride, and hover alternatives.
