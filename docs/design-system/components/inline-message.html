Inline message

One line next to the thing it explains: a hint under a field, why Start ride is off, who just left the lobby.

Overview

An inline message is one line, or a short paragraph, that explains the thing right next to it: a hint under a field, why a button is off, what changed in the lobby. It sits in the flow of the page, never floats, and never needs dismissing. The mocks use it in many small forms: .field__hint, Race Setup's .pack__note ("Around 190 W. A top 3 here earns the second star."), Custom workout's .plan-preview__blocked under a disabled Start ride, and the lobby's .race-lobby__notice ("MarcoVelo left. Their seat is open again."). .inline-message is the generic block; .field__error and .field__success are its field-sized forms.

  • Use it when the message belongs to one control or one section.
  • For a message about the whole page (a browser that cannot reach the bike), use the alert banner.
  • For a passing confirmation after an action ("Feedback sent"), use a toast.

Anatomy

  1. Icon (optional for hints, required for errors and success)14px (field) or 16px (block), the tone colour; aria-hidden.
  2. Text12px under fields, 14px in sections; the tone colour; tied to the control by aria-describedby.

States

The message itself is not interactive; it follows the state of the control it describes. Focus and hover belong to the input; a disabled field keeps its hint (dimmed with it) so the reason stays readable.

Responsive behaviour

  • Messages wrap; they never truncate (the reason is the point).
  • 360px: the field and its message take the full width; centred button reasons stay centred.
  • 640px and 1024px: field messages stay at the field's width (setup forms cap at 36rem), so lines never run past about 70 characters.

Theming

.inline-message { --inline-message-fg: var(--color-muted); --inline-message-icon: currentColor; }
.inline-message--error { --inline-message-fg: var(--color-heart-fg); }
.inline-message--warn  { --inline-message-icon: var(--color-warn-fg); }

Text uses the -fg tokens (deeper on paper), so every tone passes 4.5:1 on panel and canvas in both themes. Warn colours only the icon: amber text fails on paper.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A <p> tied to its control with aria-describedby; a field in error also sets aria-invalid="true". Messages that appear without a user action (the lobby notice) sit in role="status". An error after submit is also listed in the error summary. No APG widget; follows the ARIA21 technique.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReaches the control; the message is read as its description.

Focus

Messages never take focus. On submit, focus moves to the first invalid field, which reads its message.

Labelling

Start with what happened, then what to do ("That isn’t a YouTube link. Paste one that starts with youtube.com or youtu.be."). Icons are decoration; the words carry the tone (WCAG 1.4.1). A disabled button names its reason through aria-describedby (Start ride → "Needs the HTTPS page to reach the bike.").

Motion

None. Messages appear and disappear without animation, so nothing moves under a rider mid-ride.

Touch

Not a target. Links inside a message keep the 24px minimum through line height.

Content

  • One or two short sentences, ending with a full stop.
  • Errors: what happened, then the fix. Never "Invalid input" or "Error".
  • Name real things: the bike model, the rider, the page ("Needs the HTTPS page to reach the bike.").
  • No exclamation marks; success says what is now true ("Paired with Echelon EX-5s.").
