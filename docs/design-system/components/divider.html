Divider

Telo Ride separates with one-pixel hairlines, not boxes. A divider marks where one group ends: under a section title, between rows, between header groups, or around a short label.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableSince v1.0

Overview

The design draws almost no outlines: panels and wells separate by fill, and hairlines in --color-line separate inside them. Use a divider when two runs of content sit on the same surface and need a boundary; use spacing alone when the gap already reads (most of the time); use a panel or well when the group needs to stand apart.

  • Section rule: under a section head ("Race · All maps →"), under a shelf title, above a dialog footer. Full --color-line.
  • Row rule (--subtle): between rows of standings, the maps table, recent rides, the start grid. 60% of --color-line, so a list of ten does not turn into a cage.
  • Vertical: between groups in a header (Library | bike status; brand | bike name). 20px tall, hidden under 640px.
  • Labelled: an addition for splitting a list under a word, such as "or" between two ways to connect.

Anatomy

  1. Rule1px (--border-width-hairline) in --color-line, drawn as a top or bottom border; no margin of its own when it belongs to a head.
  2. Space12px under the head's text (--space-3), 12–16px before the next content; a standalone .divider takes 24px above and below.

Sizes

Thickness is always 1px. Spacing comes in three steps on the block: .divider 24px (between sections), --tight 12px (between rows), --flush 0 (the rule is part of something padded, like a dialog footer). The vertical rule is 20px tall (--space-5), matching a 14px text line with its leading.

States

Dividers are not interactive and have no states. They look the same in hover, focus and disabled contexts; a row's hover fill sits between its rules.

Responsive behaviour

  • Horizontal rules span their container at every width.
  • The vertical rule hides under 640px (display: none), where header groups wrap onto separate rows and a stray 20px line would float.
  • A labelled divider's label never wraps; keep it to one word.

Theming

Reads --divider-color (default --color-line; --subtle mixes it to 60%). In dark the line is on ink and panel; in light on paper. Under prefers-contrast: more the system raises --color-border-default; point --divider-color at it if a product needs dividers to strengthen too.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A rule that only decorates is a CSS border on the element it belongs to (a section head, a list row) and is invisible to assistive tech. A rule that marks a real thematic break is an <hr>, or role="separator" (with aria-orientation="vertical" for vertical ones). There is no APG widget; a static separator is not focusable (WAI-ARIA separator).

Keyboard

KeyAction
NoneDividers are skipped by Tab.

Focus

Not focusable.

Labelling

A labelled divider's word is its name (aria-label="or" on the separator); the decorative lines either side are pseudo-elements.

Contrast

Hairlines are decorative and exempt from 1.4.11; meaning never rests on one alone (the section title or the row's own text carries it). The labelled divider's word is tertiary text and passes 4.5:1.

Motion

None.

Touch

Not a target. In a list, the row (not the rule) is the target, and rows keep at least 40px height between rules.

Content

A labelled divider takes one lowercase word: "or". Anything longer is a heading.
