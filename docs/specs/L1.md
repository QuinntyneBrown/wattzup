# L1 - High-Level Requirements

Telo Ride, from the Telo family, turns a smart bike at home into a game. The browser app pairs
with the bike over Bluetooth, reads the rider's power and sets resistance for every hill and
interval. It has two doors, **Race** and **Classes**, fed by one progression of XP, rider levels,
stars and map unlocks. A thin .NET backend signs riders in, keeps their progress and runs every
ride that has more than one person in it.

These requirements are derived from the mocks in `docs/mocks/`, the source of truth for the UI.
The behaviour of existing code is not a source. Each L1 is broken down in `L2.md`.

## L1-001: Accounts and sign-in
A rider must be able to create an account and sign in with only an email address, confirmed by a
one-time six-digit code. The system must never ask for or store a password. A rider must stay
signed in on a device they keep using, choose the display name other riders see, sign out, and
delete their account.

## L1-002: Supported bikes and browsers
Telo Ride must say which bikes it works with and how well. It must work fully with the Echelon
EX-5s, EX-3, EX-8s and Connect Sport, and in beta with other bikes that speak the Bluetooth
Fitness Machine Service (FTMS). It must run in browsers that offer Web Bluetooth (Chrome and
Edge) over HTTPS. Anywhere else, it must explain why the bike cannot be reached and how to fix
it, without hiding what can still be read.

## L1-003: Bike connection
The browser app must find, pair and connect a supported bike over Bluetooth. It must remember the
bike so the rider does not pair it every time. It must keep the connection alive and recover from
drops on its own. It must always show the rider whether the bike is connected.

## L1-004: Bike telemetry
The app must turn what the bike sends into live metrics: power, cadence, speed, resistance,
distance and energy, updated about once a second. Where the bike does not report a metric (power
on an Echelon), the app must estimate it with a fixed, documented formula. A simulated bike must
let every feature run without hardware.

## L1-005: Resistance control
The app must set the bike's resistance for the rider. It follows the plan of a class, race or
custom workout, and it lets the rider nudge the resistance by hand. It must tell the target
resistance (requested) from the resistance (what the bike reports), and it must show the rider
what it sent to the bike.

## L1-006: Heart rate
The app must show heart rate when one is available: from the bike if the bike reports it, or from
a Bluetooth heart-rate strap the rider connects. Otherwise it must show that there is no reading.

## L1-007: Home
Home must answer "what should I ride now?" within ten seconds for any rider. It shows one Next
ride card that leads to the closest unlock, the Race and Classes doors, and the rider's level,
XP, streak and bike status. When a map has just opened, Home must say so.

## L1-008: Progression
Every finished ride must earn XP, and more XP for riding well. XP builds rider levels from 1 to
30. Each map has three stars to earn and one goal that opens the next map. Classes open by rider
level. Daily riding builds a streak. The backend must compute and keep all progress, so it is the
same on every device the rider signs in on.

## L1-009: Maps
Telo Ride must offer a catalogue of maps (routes in 3D worlds) in unlock order. Map Select must
show the path through the maps: which are open, which is next, and what opens each locked one.

## L1-010: Race setup
Before a race, the rider must be able to see a map's facts and stars, and choose whom to race:
their best-time ghost, a pack of bots of a chosen strength, or riders online once their level
allows it.

## L1-011: Race lobby
A race against bots or people must start from a lobby. The lobby shows the start grid of 8 to 10
seats, a start clock and a ready check. Bots fill the empty seats so every race starts full. A
shared countdown starts everyone together.

## L1-012: Racing
A race must put the rider in a 3D world on the map's route. It shows their power and position
first, then a live standings board, the current interval, the route and the resistance. It must
handle the ghost and bots, pausing (solo only), leaving, losing the bike, and finishing.

## L1-013: Results
After a race or class, the rider must see where they finished, the final standings, the XP they
earned line by line, the stars they hold, and any map their ride unlocked. The unlock is the one
moment the app celebrates with motion.

## L1-014: Classes
Telo Ride must offer a library of video classes grouped by length, each with a bike plan that
follows its video. Classes above the rider's level are locked and say the level they need. Before
riding, the rider must be able to choose to ride alone now or join the next group session.

## L1-015: Class ride
A class ride must play the class video in sync with the ride clock while the app drives the bike
through the plan. It shows the current segment's targets and the rider's output. In a group, it
also shows a board of riders ranked by output.

## L1-016: Custom workout
A rider must be able to build their own ride: interval rounds or one flat level, with a warm-up,
a cool-down and an optional video kept in sync. They must see what the ride will ask of them
before they start, then ride it on a dashboard with full manual control.

## L1-017: Profile
Profile must show the rider's level and XP, their stars and best time on every map, their recent
rides with the XP each earned, the settings for their bike and custom workouts, and the way to their ride settings.

## L1-018: Diagnostics
The rider must be able to see the state of Bluetooth, the bike and the app in one place, record
every Bluetooth message while reproducing a problem, and download that log. Logging must stay on
the device unless the rider chooses to send it.

## L1-019: Feedback
The rider must be able to send a bug report, an idea or kudos from a ride without losing their
place in it. A bug report can carry diagnostics and the ride's context. Feedback must reach the
team through the backend.

## L1-020: Multiplayer service
The backend must run every ride with more than one person in it: live races and group classes. It
seats riders, fills empty seats with bots, starts everyone together, shares each rider's progress
in real time, ranks the field, and records a result nobody can change from the browser.

## L1-021: Security and privacy
Telo Ride must protect riders' accounts and data. Every backend request must be authenticated
unless it is part of signing in. Every input must be validated and every output encoded. A rider
must see and change only their own data. Abuse must be limited. Nothing may leave the device
except to Telo Ride's backend, YouTube, and Google Maps when the rider gives their own key.

## L1-022: Performance and reliability
The app must stay accurate and responsive through a long ride. Readouts update once a second.
The clock stays true in a hidden tab, and a ride survives a dropped network. The 3D world and
the backend must meet measurable performance budgets.

## L1-023: Responsive, accessible and themed UI
Every screen must work on a phone, a tablet, a laptop and a large screen. It must work by touch
and by keyboard, meet WCAG 2.2 AA, and respect reduced motion. It is dark by default and has a
light theme. Targets must be easy to hit from the saddle.

## L1-024: Platform
The frontend must be an Angular multi-project workspace that holds most of the product, including
all Bluetooth work. The backend must be a .NET service built with clean architecture for
accounts, progress, multiplayer and feedback. Both must be tested without real hardware, real
databases or real networks, and deployed as a secure, installable web app.

## L1-025: World classes
A class can ride in a 3D world of its own. Before such a class starts, Telo Ride must get the
world ready on the rider's device and show what it is doing and how far along it is. A ride must
never start on a world that will stutter, never wait for the world when a group is starting, and
always have a way forward: a lighter quality, or the world's panorama, with the plan, the clock
and the bike working the same.

## L1-026: Ride settings
The rider must be able to tune Telo Ride for the smoothest, most pleasant ride their device can
give: the world's quality, frame rate, resolution and graphics, ride sound and music, motion,
theme, the size of the ride numbers, keeping the screen awake, and the worlds kept on the device.
Settings apply at once, even mid-ride, are kept only on that device, and can be reset to the
recommended values.

## L1-027: Courses
Each map must be a course modelled after one online class: a single 3D map whose length follows the
class's length, whose hills follow the class's resistance, and whose stages follow the class's
plan. The rider must ride the course by the class's clock, so every hill arrives with the
resistance it stands for.
