# L1 - High-Level Requirements

These requirements were derived from the existing Python/aiohttp prototype (`echelon_server.py`) and its single-page UI (`index.html`). The target implementation is an **Angular app that runs entirely in the rider's web browser**: served as static files, it talks to the bike over Web Bluetooth and keeps everything in the browser, with no Wattzup server to install or run (ADR 0019); the one exception is the optional group ride service of L1-018 (ADR 0022), a small relay that only group rides use. Behaviour from the prototype is preserved unless a requirement states otherwise.

L1-013 (treadmill) was removed when Wattzup became bike only (ADR 0016). L1-016 (a browser edition beside a backend) was folded into L1-009 and L1-012 when the backend was removed (ADR 0019).

## L1-001: Bike discovery and connection
The system must let the rider pick the nearby Echelon bike once in the browser's Bluetooth device chooser, connect to it, keep the connection alive, and recover from disconnects and errors without the rider picking it again.

## L1-002: Telemetry decoding
The system must decode the bike's notification packets into live metrics: cadence, speed (derived from cadence) and current resistance level, from which it must estimate power and the ride's average power, distance and energy, labelled as estimates.

## L1-003: Resistance control
The system must let the rider set the bike's resistance level, including overriding a running plan until its next segment, must report both the requested (target) value and the value the bike actually reports, and must log each command with its source and acknowledgement.

## L1-004: Interval workout engine
The system must run finite workout plans (HIIT intervals with an optional warm-up and cool-down, configurable interval sets where every set, the warm-up and the cool-down has its own durations, levels and cadence aims, or one flat level) made of segments, setting the bike's resistance automatically for each segment, letting the rider pause and resume, and handling the bike disconnecting and shutting off, with the workout clock kept true while the browser throttles a hidden tab.

## L1-005: Live state
The system must hold the full live state of the bike and the workout in one state model that every screen reads, updated in real time on every change, and must offer resistance and workout control to the screens through contracts that validate their input.

## L1-006: Live metrics dashboard
The app must show the rider the connection status, live estimated power against the plan's target, cadence, speed and resistance, the ride's totals, the commands sent to the bike, notifications about the connection and commands, and controls for changing resistance.

## L1-007: Workout visualisation and cues
The app must show the active workout as a countdown of the current segment with its phase, the ride's elapsed and total time, a trace of power against the plan, optionally a YouTube video the rider rides along to, played in step with the ride clock, and visual and optional audio cues at segment changes and in the final seconds of each segment. It must also let the rider set up (by building a plan or by choosing a class, L1-014), start, pause, resume, end and replace workouts.

## L1-008: Diagnostics
The system must keep a log of the Bluetooth packets that the rider can download, track how long live data has been on screen, log connection changes, commands and errors to the browser console, and show a diagnostics view for troubleshooting.

## L1-009: Security and privacy
The system must validate all input, render text from the bike, the catalogue and imported files as text, and send nothing the rider does off their device: no state, packet, session or device name leaves the browser, and the app loads nothing from other origins except, while the rider is in a group ride they chose to join (L1-018), the few ride figures that group ride shares with the group ride service, and the YouTube player for a ride-along video and the YouTube thumbnails shown as class covers (ADR 0012).

## L1-010: Performance and reliability
The system must deliver live data to the screen with low latency, use resources efficiently over long rides, and keep running through bike and Bluetooth failures.

## L1-011: Responsive and accessible UI
The app must work on every viewport size from phone to large desktop, and must be usable with a keyboard, screen readers and reduced-motion settings.

## L1-012: Platform and technology
The app must be built on Angular and delivered as static files that any HTTPS host can serve, with no Wattzup server other than the optional group ride service of L1-018, without which every other feature works as before. It must run in Chromium browsers that offer Web Bluetooth (Chrome and Edge on Windows, macOS, Linux, ChromeOS and Android), say so plainly in any other browser, be installable and work offline once loaded, and keep its Bluetooth access behind an abstraction so every feature also runs against a simulated bike.

## L1-013: Treadmill support
Removed: bike only, ADR 0016.

## L1-014: Class library
The system must keep a configurable catalogue of classes, each a bike ride plan (warm-up, intervals or a steady level, cool-down, levels and cadence aims) together with the YouTube video it follows and the point in that video where the ride starts, grouped onto shelves that the catalogue defines. Classes are data, not code: the catalogue is a file published beside the app that the owner replaces without rebuilding Wattzup (ADR 0014). The rider must be able to browse the classes by their video covers, open one to see its plan, and ride it with its configuration, changing its levels for that ride only.

## L1-015: Ride history
The system must record each finished or ended ride as a session (what was ridden, how it went and when), keep the sessions in the browser across closing and reopening it, let the rider export the history to a file and import it again, and let the rider review one session and their progress over time: what changed on a class ridden again, the streak of days ridden, a weekly goal and achievements. Only real rides are shown; nothing is invented.

## L1-017: World Ride
The frontend must let the rider ride a class inside a scene of its own, chosen for that class in the catalogue: a 3D world whose road climbs during the efforts and eases during the recoveries, so the road on screen matches the resistance the bike sets. In every scene the road bends left and right and rises and falls as a real road in that place does; the bends are scenery only, and the gradient still follows the class. The class's live metrics, current segment, route profile and resistance controls are laid over the scene. Six scenes are built in: Neon Docklands, Col du Granit, Old Town Criterium, Prairie Paceline, Dolomites and Cliffside Coast. A scene's simulated pacers are labelled as simulated and are never recorded; the real riders of a group ride (L1-018) ride beside them and are told apart from them. The scene is drawn at a quality tier fitted to the screen, from plain shading up to ray-traced light. Each world plays its own background track, a royalty-free Pixabay track that no other world uses, shipped with the app; the rider can mute it, and the choice is kept. Streaming real places as Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles is out of scope until an ADR allows it.

## L1-018: Group ride
The system must let riders ride a class together: one rider opens a group ride of a class whose scene is Old Town Criterium, others join it with its code or link under a display name they choose, and everyone starts together after a countdown. While they ride, each rider sees the others live in the scene and on the Riders nearby board, at their real gap, ahead, behind, coming up and passing, beside the simulated pacers, which stay labelled as simulated. A group ride shares only each rider's display name, jersey and ride progress (distance, speed, elapsed time and status), through a group ride service that keeps nothing once the group ride is over; a rider who loses that service keeps riding the class alone. Group ride is offered only when Wattzup is built with the address of a group ride service (ADR 0022), and other scenes than Old Town Criterium are out of scope until a requirement adds them.

## L1-019: Ride sound
The app must offer an optional ride sound that follows the bike while the rider rides: a chain that ticks with the live cadence and grows louder with the resistance, a freewheel when the pedals stop, and wind and tyre noise that rise with the speed. It is generated in the browser, with no audio file and nothing from another origin. It is off until the rider turns it on, has its own volume, gives way to the countdown beeps and to a ride-along video's own sound, and is silent while the ride is paused, the tab is hidden or the bike is lost.
