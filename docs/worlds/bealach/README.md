# Bealach na Bà
The Applecross road. A lonely, relentless Highland climb up a single-track ribbon, wind in your face and the sea loch falling away below.

## 10 critical details
1. **Single-track road** — narrow, patched tarmac with grass down the middle, no centre line
2. **Passing places** — diamond-signed lay-bys every few hundred metres, white-on-blue "Passing Place" signs
3. **Final hairpins** — the steep switchbacks under the corrie headwall near the summit, up to 20%
4. **Warning sign at the foot** — the famous sign: not advised for learner drivers or caravans
5. **Loch Kishorn** — the sea loch and its fish farms glinting far below the climb
6. **Heather and bracken** — purple heather, rust-brown bracken and bog grass on the open moor
7. **Rock buttresses and scree** — Torridonian sandstone cliffs and boulder fields above the road
8. **Summit views to Skye** — the Cuillin and Raasay across the Inner Sound from the plateau top
9. **Highland weather** — low cloud, drizzle and shafts of sunlight racing across the hills
10. **Sheep, deer and stone walls** — sheep on the road, red deer on the slopes, cattle grids and fences
