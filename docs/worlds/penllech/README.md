# Ffordd Pen Llech
Ffordd Pen Llech. A short, savage wall up a narrow Welsh street, grinding out every pedal stroke between stone cottages.

## 10 critical details
1. **The 37.45% gradient** — the street's brutal ramp, visibly tilting houses, walls and the rider's whole view.
2. **Narrow single-track lane** — barely a car wide, hemmed in by walls with no pavement.
3. **Slate and stone cottages** — grey stone Welsh terraces with slate roofs stepping up the hill.
4. **Tight hairpin bend** — the sharp left-hand bend near the top where the gradient bites hardest.
5. **Old stone walls** — mossy dry-stone and mortared walls lining both sides close enough to touch.
6. **Harlech Castle** — the great Edwardian castle on its rock, visible above the town.
7. **Snowdonia and Tremadog Bay views** — mountains and the sea and dunes of Morfa Harlech below.
8. **Bilingual signage** — Welsh and English signs, including the "Ffordd Pen Llech" name and steep-hill warnings.
9. **Spectators on the wall** — locals cheering from doorways and walls, shouting riders up the steepest metres.
10. **Welsh weather** — grey drizzle and damp road surface, with bright breaks over the sea.
